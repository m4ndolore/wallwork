Wallwork, v0. Nineteen drills, four families, MIT, ready to push.
On the "touch terms" note — I put them in the tier names, which is where they get seen most: Rookie → Solid → Sharp → Silky → Elite. "Silky" is real footballing praise for a good touch, a kid instantly gets what it means, and it gives you your own ladder rather than a borrowed one.
The three things you asked for structurally:
Drills as data. drills.json is the whole library. The engine has zero per-drill code — it reads sequence, channels, palette, footMap, tiers and runs. Adding a drill is adding an object; fixing a bad threshold is changing a number. Since you'd have to keep an inlined copy in sync by hand, there's a 12-line tools/inline-drills.mjs that does it.
One hit event. Tap, mic, and sensor all emit {source, boardId, t} onto a shared bus, and the scoring engine subscribes without knowing or caring which. The WebSocket sensor client is already stubbed in and parses {"type":"hit","board":N}. When your first ESP32 comes online you point it at the socket and nothing else changes. Multi-board is already expressible — six boards is just six values of boardId.
Setup pictures. Every drill declares a layout name and per-age distances, and one renderer draws the diagram with the right numbers baked in. Switch the age band from 7-8 to 11-12 and every diagram redraws — 4 ft becomes 6 ft, the 10 ft square becomes 12. No photos to shoot, license, or keep current, and a new drill gets a correct picture for free.
Three things I'd flag:
The open family is new — three drills that need no board at all, so your boys can start this weekend while the rebounder ships. dribble-round is the one I'd run first; it's the T4 pattern with the board removed.
Age scaling is deliberately crude — one multiplier per band, u6 at 0.4. It's a guess and it's almost certainly wrong. But it's one number in a JSON file, so after a month of sessions you'll know whether a six-year-old actually hits Solid where you expect, and fixing it is a one-line PR. That's the point of the schema.
grid-numbers and t-elite have a coaching note telling you not to run them under age eight. The numbers-beat-colours rule inversion is genuinely too abstract at that age, and a drill a kid can't succeed at is worse than no drill.
For the ESP32 you'll want the accelerometer on the back of the panel near the centre, not the frame — the frame damps the transient and you'll fight the threshold forever. Say the word when the rebounder lands and we'll do the firmware and the enclosure.
